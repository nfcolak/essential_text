## 3. Research Question Traceability Matrix (`Method fits` & `Analysis`)

To ensure every research question in Section 1 is directly answered by the datasets, baseline tiers, and statistical tests in Sections 2 and 3, the following traceability mapping governs the proposal:

| Research Question | Primary Hypothesis | Models / Tiers Compared | Target Metrics & Phenomenon Slice | Statistical Test / Verification Criterion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RQ1:** *To what extent do general-purpose sentiment methods misclassify financial language, particularly by confusing neutral financial terminology with negative sentiment?* | General-domain lexicons (VADER) and encoders (`bert-base-uncased`) exhibit systematically elevated false-negative errors on `neutral` sentences containing descriptive accounting terms (*cost, liability, depreciation, debt*), reducing `neutral` recall ($R_{\text{neu}}$) and `negative` precision ($P_{\text{neg}}$). | • VADER [6] vs. Loughran–McDonald [2]<br>• `bert-base-uncased` [4] vs. FinBERT-MLM [3] | • $3 \times 3$ Confusion Matrix cell $C_{\text{neu} \to \text{neg}}$<br>• Neutral Recall ($R_{\text{neu}}$) & Negative Precision ($P_{\text{neg}}$)<br>• Phenomenon Slice 1 (*Financial Terminology*) | • McNemar's test on `neutral` class error rates<br>• Paired bootstrap 95% CI on $\Delta R_{\text{neu}}$ and $\Delta P_{\text{neg}}$ ($B = 10{,}000$) |
| **RQ2:** *Under a strictly leakage-free protocol, how much does financial domain adaptation improve classification performance over traditional ML and general-domain BERT—both in-domain and out-of-distribution?* | When fine-tuned from pre-sentiment MLM weights on identical locked PhraseBank splits, FinBERT-MLM significantly outperforms `bert-base-uncased` and TF-IDF + LR/SVM in Macro-F1, and maintains its advantage zero-shot on deduplicated SEntFiN 1.0. | • Tier 1: VADER, LM<br>• Tier 2: TF-IDF + LR, Linear SVM<br>• Tier 3: `bert-base-uncased` vs. pre-sentiment FinBERT-MLM (Stage 1) + `ProsusAI/finbert` on SEntFiN 1.0 (Stage 2) | • **Macro-F1** (primary) & Accuracy across PhraseBank (`allagree`, `75agree`, `50agree`) and SEntFiN 1.0 ($N=2{,}000$) | • Pairwise McNemar's test across all model pairs (Holm–Bonferroni adjusted)<br>• Paired bootstrap 95% CI on $\Delta\text{Macro-F1}$ ($B = 10{,}000$)<br>• Mean $\pm$ SD across 3 seeds & 5 CV folds |
| **RQ3:** *Which specific linguistic phenomena drive performance differences across model families, and how much of FinBERT's gain stems from domain-adaptive pretraining vs. the Transformer architecture?* | Transformer architecture ($\Delta_{\text{arch}} = \text{BERT}_{\text{base}} - \text{TF-IDF+SVM}$) drives gains on compositional phenomena (*negation*, *context-dependent expressions*), whereas financial domain pretraining ($\Delta_{\text{domain}} = \text{FinBERT}_{\text{MLM}} - \text{BERT}_{\text{base}}$) drives gains on *financial terminology*, *hedging/modality*, and *numerical comparisons*. | • All 3 tiers decomposed via $\Delta_{\text{arch}, k}$ and $\Delta_{\text{domain}, k}$ across $k \in \{1,\dots,5\}$ | • Per-phenomenon Macro-F1 and error rate across the 5 categories on the 300-sentence dual-annotated diagnostic set | • Inter-annotator reliability (Cohen's $\kappa \ge 0.75$)<br>• Phenomenon-stratified bootstrap 95% CIs on $\Delta_{\text{arch}, k}$ and $\Delta_{\text{domain}, k}$ |
